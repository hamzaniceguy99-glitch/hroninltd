Program 01 · Assessed, then made

Plastic product manufacture

The sequence matters: assess the hazards, compile the technical file, draw up the declaration, then produce and mark. Done the other way round, a problem found later means a withdrawal rather than a design change. Material choice is part of the assessment, not separate from it, because chemical restrictions apply to what the product is made of.

Program 02 · The reason, not just an age

Games and toys

A toy excluded from under-threes must say what the hazard is, not simply carry a number. Small parts and choking are the usual reason and parents deserve to be told that specifically, because a generic age label gets read as a developmental suggestion rather than a safety warning. Button cells sit behind a compartment needing a tool.

Program 03 · Distributor duties too

In store and online

Where we sell someone else's product, the manufacturer's duties are theirs — but a distributor still has to check the marking, documentation and warnings are present and must not supply something it has reason to believe does not conform. Plenty of stock available wholesale fails that check, and the answer is not to list it.

Program 04 · So it can be found

Traceability and recall

Traceability is only ever needed on a bad day, which is exactly why it has to exist on the good ones. Our name and address plus a batch or type number go on the product or packaging, and we keep records of who supplied us and who we supplied. If something does not conform we take corrective action and notify the authority rather than hoping it was a one-off.
